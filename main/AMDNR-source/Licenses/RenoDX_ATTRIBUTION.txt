RenoDX
======

Project:  RenoDX by clshortfuse
Source:   https://github.com/clshortfuse/renodx

What is derived from it
-----------------------

Three places in this build use RenoDX's maths: two compose the neural model's answer,
and one decodes it:

  * OptiScaler/shaders/dlssnr/precompile/dlssnr.hlsl -- the NVIDIA path.

  * OptiScaler/dlssnr/amd/NrCompose.h -- the AMD path's "RenoDX (experimental)"
    colour composition ([DlssNr] AmdComposition = 1), one shared pass used by
    both AMD runtimes, danielblnc's and lmxxf's. It is a port of the tail of
    dlssnr.hlsl's composition; its helpers are copied from that file.

  * OptiScaler/dlssnr/amd/AmdPreSr.cpp, ProxyShader -- the decode of the
    danielblnc runtime's Highlight proxy ([DlssNr] AmdHighlightProxy = 1). It
    divides the model's answer by the encode's scale recomputed from the
    ORIGINAL pixel: the principle of RenoDX's DLSS addon N2 colour bridge
    (n2 = Neutwo(peak)/peak from the original, decoded g/n2). The principle
    only; no RenoDX code is used there.

Specifically, from RenoDX:

  * UpgradeToneMap -- the two-branch luminance ratio: below the proxy's luminance
    the original's own luminance is the target, and above it the difference is
    treated as headroom the proxy could not represent and handed back on top of
    the model's answer. The source is RenoDX's shader library,
    renodx::tonemap::config::UpgradeToneMap in src/shaders/tonemap.hlsl, which
    computes that ratio, then renodx::color::correct::Hue, then a lerp by the
    post-process strength. Our port differs in three small ways: it returns the
    original where the model's luminance is at or below 1e-5 (RenoDX uses a
    ratio of 0 where the graded luminance is not positive), it leaves out
    RenoDX's auto-correction term (0 by default there), and it floors the
    proxy's luminance at 1e-6.

  * The OkLab hue correction applied to the rescaled result
    (renodx::color::correct::Hue), which takes the hue and chroma direction from
    the model's own output and only the chroma magnitude from the scaled colour.

  * The reversible neutral-axis gamut compression, from RenoDX's DLSS 5 Neural
    Rendering addon (the same constants): the D65 adaptation state carried in a
    Hunt-Pointer-Estevez LMS basis, the largest scale toward the neutral colour
    of equal luminance that leaves no channel negative, and the compression
    itself. Ours previously clamped per channel in AP1, which bends the hue of
    exactly the saturated pixels most likely to breach; theirs scales the whole
    colour by one factor so only saturation gives way, and is the identity when
    nothing is out of gamut. In both shaders the function keeps its historical
    name, ClampAp1; nothing in it clamps in AP1 any more.

  * The Neutwo reversible proxy in dlssnr.hlsl ("Reversible proxy
    (experimental)" on the NVIDIA path), from RenoDX's Sep-2 DLSS 5 addon: an
    unclipped, hue-preserving encode, x / sqrt(x^2 + 1) applied as one scalar on
    the peak channel.

On the AMD path the two-branch ratio and the hue step run where the proxy
exists. On lmxxf's runtime the same two steps run inside lmxxf's codec, against
the exact proxy the network saw; on danielblnc's closed runtime the runtime
composes its own answer. NrCompose.h then runs the rest on both: detail, the
guard, the colour blend, the gamut compression and the skin / environment edit.

This is their design. It was reimplemented here with different names and
comments, which does not make it original work, and it is credited as theirs.

What is not derived from it
---------------------------

  * The final blend between a luminance-only result (the original's hue at the
    model's light) and the fully upgraded colour -- the Colour strength control
    -- was not found in RenoDX. It is part of NVIDIA's "mode 1" codec as
    reconstructed by lmxxf (https://github.com/lmxxf/dlss5-on-amd-9070xt-porting,
    MIT; native_codec_decode.hlsl), and is credited to that reconstruction.
    Earlier versions of this file listed it under RenoDX. lmxxf's licence is in
    lmxxf_LICENSE.txt in the release's Licenses folder.

  * The 1/512 ratio floor, the power amplification above strength 1, the
    two-sided highlight guard and the OkLab chroma boost above strength 1 come
    from the dlssnr.hlsl lineage this build descends from: OptiScaler ->
    Dagherbou / OptiScaler_DLSSNR -> wilsjo2 / OptiScaler-DLSSNR-PreSR-Multipass.
    The skin-colour selection (SkinColourWeight) with its separate skin and
    environment controls is wilsjo2's.

  * The OkLab conversion matrices are Bjorn Ottosson's published constants. The
    sRGB transfer functions and the BT.709 luminance weights are standard colour
    science and are not specific to RenoDX.

  * AMDNR (3zwr1) wrote only the AMD integration: the shared pass in
    NrCompose.h and its use on both runtimes, the white-point handling, and
    lmxxf's matched lift. The RDNA 3 backend is also AMDNR's and has nothing to
    do with RenoDX.

  * The danielblnc Highlight proxy's curve (a shifted Reinhard knee, not
    Neutwo), its knee, the hand-over low in the curve to the model's own answer
    decoded through the curve, and the 3x3 bound are AMDNR's (3zwr1).
